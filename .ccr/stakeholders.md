# Stakeholders

## Direct

- **Students.** They want to join courses, finish assignments, and understand how they are doing.
  They create an account, enroll themselves, write and revise responses, and read scores, comments,
  current grades, and study suggestions. They rely on deadlines, grades, and feedback being clear
  and accurate. According to the project owner, current users enter fictional details, and grades
  carry no academic weight.
- **Instructors.** Each one teaches one assigned course. They read submitted work, publish scores
  and comments, see which students may need a check-in, and review course-wide results. Their
  judgment sets every score. Fixed rules shape the grade, the flags, and the engagement groups they
  see.

## Indirect

- **Students who are flagged or grouped.** Instructors see support flags and engagement groups,
  but students are not told when they are flagged. A flag may lead an instructor to reach out, and
  the student has no say in how that conversation is framed.
- **People represented in future equity reports.** The owner plans reports that compare outcomes
  across gender, ethnicity, and race. Those reports do not exist yet. The students they describe
  would be affected by how groups are defined and by the decisions those reports inform, even if
  they never see the reports.
- **Whoever runs the app.** Someone has to install and start it on a computer and keep its helper
  program running. They can also wipe all work in that browser, as can anyone signed in.

## Potentially affected

- **Students who join a course late.** All past-due assignments count as zero until submitted, so
  they usually start with a low grade, a support flag for their instructor, and study suggestions
  aimed at struggling students. They carry extra catch-up work. The app warns them before they
  enroll.
- **Students who revise after a deadline.** A new version replaces the old one and is marked late,
  even if the first version was on time.
- **Students outside the app's time zone.** All times appear on one world-standard clock, so
  deadlines may differ from what they expect in local time.
- **People who share a computer.** Because there are no passwords, anyone at the same computer can
  open another person's account. They can read or change that person's work, profile, or grades,
  or act as an instructor.
- **People who use assistive technology, or do not read English well.** The interface is English
  only. Key explanations are tucked behind small information buttons. Full keyboard and
  screen-reader use has not been confirmed.
- **Students whose identities do not fit the listed demographic options.** Gender identity offers
  a self-description. Ethnicity and race offer fixed lists plus "prefer not to answer". All three
  questions are optional.
- **Students whose work is not plain text,** such as files, images, or recordings. The app accepts
  only a typed response.

## Roles and access

- Students change only their own profile, drafts, and submissions, and only until work is graded.
  They cannot delete their account or dispute a score in the app. They are told to contact their
  instructor, but no route for that contact has been confirmed.
- Instructors decide scores and comments for their own course and can correct them at any time.
  They cannot change the grading rules, the 70% flag threshold, or the engagement groups.
- No one can change the fixed rules for grades, flags, engagement groups, or study suggestions
  from inside the app.
- Access is chosen from a list rather than proven, and information stays only in the browser where
  it was entered. Anyone signed in can restore the original sample records, which erases everyone's
  new work in that browser.

## Impacted workflows

- **Enroll and submit (students):** this decides when work counts and whether it is marked late.
  For late joiners, it also adds immediate zeros and extra workload.
- **Grade and give feedback (instructors):** scores take effect for the student right away. With
  no scoring guide or second review, results depend on one instructor's judgment. Instructors can
  correct mistakes.
- **Support check-ins (instructors and students):** a below-70% grade prompts outreach that the
  app describes as supportive, not punitive. Students do not see that the prompt exists. This can
  shape how they are approached.
- **Course analytics (instructors):** completion-based engagement groups are a rough measure and
  may label students who have caught up late or who participate in other ways as "low engagement".
- **Study suggestions (students):** suggestions are based on grade bands and assignment keywords.
  They are optional and do not affect grades. They may steer late joiners toward remedial material.
- **Profile and demographics (students):** optional and unused today. Planned group comparisons
  would make how these answers are collected, kept, and shared matter more.
